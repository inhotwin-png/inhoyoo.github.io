01 / Research experience

Completed introductory catalog analysis

Spatial distribution of SPLICES target candidates

A spatial view of the SPHEREx / SPLICES target catalog.

In the Matsuura Laboratory, I analyzed the SPLICES catalog of background sources selected for infrared absorption spectroscopy of interstellar ice. The 3.1 GB FITS catalog contains approximately 9 million sources.

Using Python and Astropy’s memory-mapped FITS access, I randomly sampled 10,000 sources and plotted their right ascension and declination to examine the catalog’s spatial distribution.

Python · Astropy · NumPy · Pandas · Matplotlib

Data source: I downloaded the FITS catalog from the NASA/IPAC Infrared Science Archive (IRSA) SPLICES archive. The code below uses 20241107_SPLICESv8.6.fits. Dataset DOI: 10.26131/IRSA554; catalog reference: Ashby et al. (2023).

04 / Proposed research

Research proposal · Not yet implemented or validated

OASIS

Optimal Asteroid Scoring via Infrared and X-ray Spectroscopy

My long-term motivation is to support space-resource exploration by developing methods that assess composition and its uncertainty, helping prioritize promising targets for further investigation.

I propose a multimodal scientific machine-learning framework to study planetary surface composition using heterogeneous infrared and X-ray observations. The central question is whether complementary measurements can support compositional inference when one sensing modality is noisy or unavailable.

A proposed Moon → Bennu → Ryugu progression would explore complementary, noisy, and missing observations, with comparisons to returned-sample measurements where appropriate. Data availability, spatial correspondence, and transfer between planetary bodies would need to be established before validation.

The diagrams and AI-generated concept illustrations below outline proposed studies. Labels such as “robustness verified” and “PoC completed” in the original illustrations describe intended outcomes, not completed results. No trained model, validated composition map, or completed proof of concept is presented. Compositional screening would inform further exploration; it would not establish resource recoverability or economic feasibility.

OASIS / Phase 1 · Proposed

Moon — Establish a baseline

Explore a baseline model combining lunar infrared observations, X-ray measurements, and sample composition. Deliberately masking X-ray inputs would test sensitivity to missing information and define a screening procedure for later experiments.

OASIS / Phase 2 · Proposed

Bennu — Study multimodal learning under noise

Investigate infrared observations as the primary input, with X-ray measurements considered only if suitable data are available. An initial feasibility study would assess data quality and spatial correspondence before testing whether complementary measurements improve compositional inference.

OASIS / Phase 3 · Proposed

Ryugu — Evaluate transfer with a missing modality

Test whether a trained model can generalize to Ryugu using infrared observations without an X-ray input. Proposed comparisons with Hayabusa2 sample analyses would assess compositional consistency, accounting for the limited spatial correspondence between remote observations and returned samples.
