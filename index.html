01 / Current research

Ongoing project · Completed catalog analysis

Mapping targets for cosmic ice exploration

A spatial view of the SPHEREx / SPLICES target catalog.

In the Matsuura Laboratory, I analyzed the SPLICES catalog of background sources selected for infrared absorption spectroscopy of interstellar ice. The 3.1 GB FITS catalog contains approximately 9 million sources.

I built a memory-efficient Python pipeline using Astropy’s memory-mapped FITS access, randomly sampled 10,000 sources to examine the large-scale distribution, and plotted their right ascension and declination.

Python · Astropy · NumPy · Pandas · Matplotlib

02 / Research result

Completed spatial mapping

The Galactic plane in equatorial coordinates

A random sample reveals a coherent, nonuniform distribution across the sky.

The Galactic plane

The curved, S-shaped concentration traces the Galactic plane in right ascension and declination. Its shape reflects the inclination of the Galactic plane relative to the celestial equator.

Toward the Galactic center

The enhanced concentration toward Sagittarius is consistent with the Galactic-center direction and SPLICES selection of molecular-cloud-rich sightlines. The figure shows the distribution of selected background targets; it does not directly measure ice abundance.

03 / Next research step

Planned work

From spatial mapping to Bennu spectroscopy

Move from where the targets are to what their spectra can tell us.

My next step is to develop and validate Python spectral-fitting routines using public OSIRIS-REx infrared observations of Bennu. The aim is to characterize absorption features associated with water and hydrated minerals, and assess the reliability of their fitted depths and shapes.

This work would build experience in spectral analysis for subsequent SPHEREx studies. The catalog map above is complete; Bennu spectral fitting and compositional interpretation remain planned work.

04 / Proposed research

Research proposal · Not yet implemented or validated

OASIS

Optimal Asteroid Scoring via Infrared and X-ray Spectroscopy

I propose a multimodal scientific machine-learning framework to study planetary surface composition using heterogeneous infrared and X-ray observations. The central question is whether complementary measurements can support compositional inference when one sensing modality is noisy or unavailable.

A Moon → Bennu → Ryugu progression would examine complete, noisy, and missing modalities, with proposed comparisons to returned-sample measurements. Data availability, spatial correspondence, and transfer between planetary bodies would need to be established before validation.

The original AI-generated concept images below illustrate intended experiments and outcomes. Labels such as “robustness verified” and “PoC completed” describe aspirations in the illustrations, not achieved results.

OASIS / Phase 1 · Proposed

Moon — Establish a baseline

Explore a baseline model combining lunar infrared observations, X-ray measurements, and sample composition. Deliberately masking X-ray inputs would test sensitivity to missing information and define a screening procedure for later experiments.

OASIS / Phase 2 · Proposed

Bennu — Study multimodal learning under noise

Investigate a model using infrared observations as the primary input and available X-ray observations as an auxiliary modality. The proposed experiment would evaluate sensitivity to solar-activity-related noise and investigate inference of hydrated minerals and organic material.

OASIS / Phase 3 · Proposed

Ryugu — Evaluate transfer with a missing modality

Test whether a trained model can generalize to Ryugu using infrared observations without an X-ray input. Proposed comparisons with Hayabusa2 sample analyses would assess compositional consistency, accounting for the limited spatial correspondence between remote observations and returned samples.
